CPA ethics CPE requirements by state

Updated November 19, 2026

Every state but South Dakota sets an ethics requirement, and many only accept a course approved by their own board or one that covers their state's laws and rules. Hours below are as each state's rules state them; open a state for the full picture.

StateEthics requirementDetails
Alabama2 hours each calendar yearAt least 2 hours in a qualifying ethics course every year.
Alaska4 hours every 2 years4 hours of ethics each period (12 AAC 04.300(d)). Any approved ethics course counts; no Alaska-specific course is required.
Arizona4 hours every 2 years4 hours, including at least 1 hour on the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct and 1 hour on Arizona Board statutes and rules. Not taught by your employer or a co-worker.
Arkansas4 hours over 3 calendar years4 hours of ethics every 36 months, including 1 hour on Arkansas Board laws and rules (the Board's free "Arkansas Board Laws and Rules" course counts).
California4 hours every 2 years4 hours of technical ethics (codes of conduct, ethical dilemmas, case studies). Only Regulatory Ethics counts; Behavioral Ethics is non-technical in California and doesn't count toward these 4.
Colorado4 hours every 2 years4 hours of ethics; up to 2 of them can be Colorado Rules & Regulations (CR&R) — more CR&R doesn't count as ethics. Regulatory and behavioral ethics both count.
Connecticut4 hours over 3 calendar years4 hours of ethics in every three CPE years.
Delaware4 hours every 2 yearsA 4-hour Board-approved Delaware ethics course each period (§7.2.6).
Florida4 hours every 2 years4 hours from a Florida Board-approved ethics provider, covering Chapters 455 and 473, F.S.
Georgia4 hours every 2 years; including 1 hour every 2 years1 of the 4 ethics credits must cover the laws, rules and policies of the Georgia Board.
Hawaii4 hours every 2 yearsAt least 4 hours of ethics or professional conduct each biennium. Extra ethics counts as regular CPE but doesn't carry as ethics.
Idaho4 hours over 2 calendar years 
Illinois4 hours every 3 years 
Indiana4 hours every 3 years4 hours of ethics each period (not prorated except for a certificate issued in the last quarter).
Iowa4 hours over 3 calendar yearsAt least 4 hours of ethics and rules of professional conduct per three years.
Kansas2 hours every 2 years2 hours of professional ethics relating to CPA practice each period.
Kentucky2 hours every 2 years2 hours of ethics each period. Courses don't have to be Kentucky-specific.
Louisiana3 hours in even-numbered yearsIn even-numbered years, a Board-approved Louisiana ethics course (3 hours in 2026). General ethics doesn't count.
Maine4 hours over 3 calendar yearsAt least 4 hours of ethics every three years: Maine accountancy laws and rules, or the AICPA or SEC conduct standards (Ch. 5 §8(B)).
Maryland4 hours every 2 years4 hours of professional ethics every license period. Extra ethics hours count as general CPE but can't carry forward as ethics.
Massachusetts4 hours every 2 years4 hours of professional ethics each renewal (252 CMR 2.14). Massachusetts doesn't require a state-specific course.
Michigan2 hours each year; including 1 hour every 2 years1 of the 4 ethics hours in each two-year cycle must cover Michigan's accountancy statutes and rules (the MICPA course).
Minnesota8 hours over 3 calendar years8 hours of regulatory or behavioral ethics in the rolling three CPE years.
Mississippi3 hours over 3 calendar years3 hours of Board-approved ethics and professional conduct each triennium (now July 2025 – June 2028).
Missouri2 hours each calendar yearAt least 2 of the 40 hours each year in ethics. Regulatory and behavioral ethics both count.
Montana2 hours over 3 calendar years2 hours of ethics in the three years. No Montana-specific course is required.
Nebraska4 hours every 2 years4 hours of ethics each period (general or professional ethics, independence, Circular 230 and similar).
Nevada2 hours each calendar year2 hours of professional ethics each year.
New Hampshire4 hours every 2 yearsAll licensees: at least 4 hours of professional ethics each period (Ac 403.01(d)). No New Hampshire-specific course is required.
New Jersey4 hours every 3 yearsThe Board-approved 4-hour New Jersey Law & Ethics course, taken in person or by live webinar from an approved provider.
New Mexico4 hours every 3 years4 hours of ethics in the 36 months (AICPA code, New Mexico code of conduct, Circular 230 and similar).
New York4 hours over 3 calendar yearsIf New York is your principal place of business, only an ethics course approved by NYSED is acceptable.
North Carolina1 hour each calendar yearAt least 50 minutes a year of regulatory or behavioral professional ethics from a NASBA-registered sponsor. Carried-forward hours can't meet it.
North Dakota6 hours over 3 calendar years6 hours of ethics in the rolling three reporting years. No North Dakota-specific course is required.
Ohio3 hours every 3 years3 credits each three-year period from a course approved by the Board as Professional Standards and Responsibilities — it can cover Ohio accountancy law and rules, professional ethics, ethical philosophy, or another state's accountancy laws.
Oklahoma4 hours over 3 calendar yearsAt least 4 hours of ethics in each rolling three-year period.
Oregon4 hours every 2 years4 hours of Oregon-specific ethics each period (Oregon statutes and rules, the Board's Code of Professional Conduct). If your principal office is in another state with an ethics requirement, those hours can count instead.
Pennsylvania4 hours every 2 years 
Rhode Island6 hours every 3 yearsAt least 6 hours (4 in your first cycle) covering regulatory and behavioral ethics (§1.8.2(E)). No Rhode Island-specific course is required.
South Carolina2 hours each calendar yearAt least 2 hours of ethics every year. Since 2022 any behavioral or regulatory ethics course counts.
South DakotaNo separate ethics requirement 
Tennessee2 hours every 2 yearsA 2-hour Board-approved Tennessee state-specific ethics course every two-year period.
Texas4 hours in the last 24 monthsA 4-hour TSBPA-approved ethics course every two years. Out-of-state licensees with no Texas clients may meet their home state's ethics rule instead.
Utah4 hours every 2 years4 hours of ethics each cycle, including the 1-hour Utah laws and rules course.
Vermont4 hours every 2 yearsAt least 4 hours of ethics for accountants each period (Rule 9.1(e)).
Virginia2 hours each calendar yearA VBOA-approved ethics course of at least 2 hours every calendar year (it includes the VBOA segment).
Washington4 hours every 3 yearsA 4-hour ethics and regulation course from a Washington Board-approved provider. Other ethics courses count toward the 120 but not this.
West Virginia4 hours over 3 calendar yearsAt least 4 hours of ethics in the three years (ethical reasoning, West Virginia statutes and rules, professional conduct).
Wisconsin3 hours every 2 years3 hours of ethics from formal learning activities each period. Regulatory and behavioral ethics both count.
Wyoming4 hours over 3 calendar yearsThe Board-approved 4-hour Wyoming ethics course (Wyoming statutes and rules, 80% to pass) once every three years, and within 6 months of first certification.

What to watch for

  • State-specific courses. Florida, Texas, Washington, Virginia, New Jersey and others require a course approved by their own board or covering their own laws. A general AICPA ethics course may not count.
  • Regulatory vs. behavioral ethics. NASBA classes Regulatory Ethics as technical and Behavioral Ethics as non-technical. Some states, such as California, only count regulatory ethics toward their ethics hours.
  • Timing. Some states want ethics every year, others once per two- or three-year period. Check the period, not just the hours.
