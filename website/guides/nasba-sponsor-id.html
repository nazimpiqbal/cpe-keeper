How to find and check a NASBA sponsor ID

Updated November 19, 2026

Most state boards accept CPE from sponsors on NASBA's National Registry of CPE Sponsors, and several ask you to record the sponsor's ID. Every Registry sponsor gets an ID number, and it belongs on every certificate it issues.

Where to find it

Look on the certificate of completion. NASBA requires Registry sponsors to print their Registry sponsor identification number and a Registry statement on every certificate, alongside:

  • the sponsor's name and address, and your name;
  • the program title, field(s) of study, date and location;
  • the delivery method (for example Group Live, Group Internet Based or QAS Self Study) and the credit earned;
  • the signature of the person responsible for the sponsor's CPE programs.

How to check a sponsor

  1. Find the sponsor in NASBA's National Registry sponsor list, by name or ID.
  2. Confirm its status for your course date with NASBA's "Confirm Registry CPE Sponsor Status" tool. It asks for the sponsor ID, the delivery method and the program date, and shows whether the sponsor was registered for that method on that date.
  3. Check the delivery method. A sponsor may be registered for some methods and not others. Self-study must come from a sponsor approved for QAS Self Study.

The Registry approves sponsors, not individual courses, and a Registry listing doesn't guarantee your state accepts a course. Your state's rules still decide.

If the ID is missing

  • Ask the sponsor for a corrected certificate. Registry sponsors are required to include it.
  • Some sponsors aren't on the Registry. Many states still accept their courses if they meet the state's standards, but some limit them. Texas, for example, asks you to justify credit from non-registered sponsors and caps it at half your hours.
  • Some states have their own sponsor numbers too. New York asks you to keep the sponsor's New York State sponsor number, and Texas the sponsor's identification number.
