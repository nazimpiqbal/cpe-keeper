Technical vs. non-technical CPE

Updated November 19, 2026

Every CPE course is assigned one or more NASBA fields of study, printed on the certificate. NASBA groups the 20 fields into technical and non-technical, and many states cap non-technical hours or require a minimum of technical ones.

NASBA's fields of study

Technical (13)

  • Accounting
  • Accounting (Governmental)
  • Auditing
  • Auditing (Governmental)
  • Business Law
  • Economics
  • Finance
  • Information Technology
  • Management Services
  • Regulatory Ethics
  • Specialized Knowledge
  • Statistics
  • Taxes

Non-technical (7)

  • Behavioral Ethics
  • Business Management & Organization
  • Communications and Marketing
  • Computer Software & Applications
  • Personal Development
  • Personnel/Human Resources
  • Production

Note that Computer Software & Applications is non-technical, while Information Technology is technical, and that the two halves of ethics fall on different sides.

States with a technical minimum

These states set a minimum number of technical hours. Others limit specific non-technical areas, such as personal development, or don't split technical and non-technical at all; each state page has the details.

When your state classifies a course differently

A state board can define technical subjects its own way. NASBA's guidance lets a sponsor reissue a certificate under a different field of study when needed to match your state's accepted fields, as long as the new field reasonably reflects the course content. If a course you took doesn't fit your state's categories, ask the sponsor.
