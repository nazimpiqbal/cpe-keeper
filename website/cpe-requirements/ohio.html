Ohio CPE requirements for CPAs

Checked against the board's rules on October 1, 2026

Board
Accountancy Board of Ohio
CPE required
120 hours every 3 years
Renewal
Ohio reporting periods end December 31 (e.g. 12/31/2027). New licensees: Dec 31 of the year after you were certified.
Carryover
None

How it works

Each permit's triennial reporting period runs January 1 to December 31 three years later.

Requirements

Overall requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Total CPE120every 3 years 
Total hours each year20each yearAt least 20 credits by December 31 each year — $10 fine per missing credit.

Subject requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Accounting, auditing & attest24every 3 yearsApplies to: Accounting/audit/attest work or financial reports. 24 credits in accounting, auditing or attestation standards each three-year period if you perform accounting, auditing, assurance or attestation engagements, or prepare or sign any financial report (OAC 4701-15-11(A)).
Taxation24every 3 yearsApplies to: Tax work or tax returns. 24 credits in taxation each three-year period if you do tax work on any engagement, or prepare or sign any tax return (OAC 4701-15-11(B)).

Special requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Professional Standards & Responsibilities (Ohio)3every 3 years3 credits each three-year period from a course approved by the Board as Professional Standards and Responsibilities — it can cover Ohio accountancy law and rules, professional ethics, ethical philosophy, or another state's accountancy laws.

New licensees

Initial period: 40 credits from January 1 of the year you were certified through December 31 of the next year — no yearly minimum or subject requirements.

Other limits

  • Instruction: max 90 credits per period (OAC 4701-15-04).
  • Live group study: max 8 credits in one day.
  • Professional exams: max 40 credits per session; election official service: max 20 per period.

Licensed in more than one state?

Many boards let CPAs whose main office is in another state meet CPE through their home state's rules instead, sometimes with extras such as a state ethics course. Check with the Accountancy Board of Ohio whether that applies to you.
