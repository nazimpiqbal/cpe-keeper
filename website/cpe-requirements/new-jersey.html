New Jersey CPE requirements for CPAs

Checked against the board's rules on October 1, 2026

Board
New Jersey State Board of Accountancy (Division of Consumer Affairs)
CPE required
120 hours every 3 years
Renewal
New Jersey licenses renew by December 31 at the end of each triennial period (currently 12/31/2026).
Carryover
None

How it works

One triennial period for everyone (currently Jan 1, 2024 – Dec 31, 2026); licenses renew by December 31 at the end of it.

Requirements

Overall requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Total CPE120every 3 years120 credits over the triennial period, at least 20 each calendar year. Courses on developing your practice or marketing services don't count in New Jersey (N.J.A.C. 13:29-6.4) — leave them out.
Total hours each year20each year 

Subject requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Technical subjects60every 3 yearsAt least 60 of the 120 in accounting, auditing (including review, compilation and attest), business law, information technology, economics, finance, management advisory services, mathematics/statistics, SEC practice, taxation or professional ethics (N.J.A.C. 13:29-6.3). The New Jersey Law & Ethics course counts here too.
Accounting & auditing (public practice)24every 3 yearsApplies to: In public practice. 24 hours in accounting or auditing (review and compilation count) if you're in the practice of public accountancy.

Special requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
New Jersey Law & Ethics4every 3 yearsThe Board-approved 4-hour New Jersey Law & Ethics course, taken in person or by live webinar from an approved provider.

New licensees

First renewal after licensure: the 120 / 20-per-year / technical / A&A requirements don't apply. The 4-hour New Jersey Law & Ethics course still does.

Other limits

  • Instruction: max 60 hours.
  • Authorship: max 30 hours.
  • Reviewer credit: max 60 hours.

Licensed in more than one state?

Many boards let CPAs whose main office is in another state meet CPE through their home state's rules instead, sometimes with extras such as a state ethics course. Check with the New Jersey State Board of Accountancy (Division of Consumer Affairs) whether that applies to you.
