Virginia CPE requirements for CPAs

Checked against the board's rules on October 4, 2026

Board
Virginia Board of Accountancy (VBOA)
CPE required
120 hours over 3 calendar years
Renewal
Virginia licenses renew every year by June 30. CPE is tracked by calendar year regardless.
Carryover
None
Record keeping
Keep CPE documentation for the four calendar years before the current year.

How it works

Virginia checks CPE by calendar year: 120 hours over the rolling three calendar years ending this year, at least 20 hours and a 2-hour VBOA-approved ethics course every year. You have until January 31 to finish the previous year's hours.

Requirements

Overall requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Three-year total120over 3 calendar yearsThe three calendar years ending this year must reach 120 hours (18VAC5-22-90).
Minimum each year20each calendar yearAt least 20 hours every calendar year, including the ethics course.

Subject requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Attest, compilation or financial statement preparation8each calendar yearApplies to: Attest, compilation or financial statement preparation. If you provide attest, compilation or financial statement preparation services: at least 8 hours a year related to those services.

Special requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
VBOA-approved ethics2each calendar yearA VBOA-approved ethics course of at least 2 hours every calendar year (it includes the VBOA segment).

New licensees

Licensed this calendar year: you're considered to have met this year's CPE (18VAC5-22-90 B).

Other limits

  • Presentations and teaching: max 30 hours per three-year period.
  • Published materials: max 30 hours per three-year period.
  • Certifications and exams: max 60 hours per three-year period.
  • January grace: hours finished by January 31 can count for the previous year (each course counts toward one year only) — enter them as you report them.

Licensed in more than one state?

Many boards let CPAs whose main office is in another state meet CPE through their home state's rules instead, sometimes with extras such as a state ethics course. Check with the Virginia Board of Accountancy (VBOA) whether that applies to you.
