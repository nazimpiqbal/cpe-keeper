Florida CPE requirements for CPAs

Checked against the board's rules on October 2, 2026

Board
Florida Board of Accountancy (DBPR)
CPE required
80 hours every 2 years
Renewal
Florida CPE periods end June 30 every two years (e.g. 06/30/2027). This is separate from your license renewal date.
Carryover
None
Record keeping
Keep proof for at least two years after each two-year period.

How it works

Florida CPE periods run July 1 – June 30, every two years.

Requirements

Overall requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Total CPE80every 2 yearsHours must be done by June 30. Proof must be uploaded to the DBPR On-Line Services portal by December 31 after the period, before your license renewal. Nonresidents may instead show their home state's CPE compliance, but still need the Florida ethics course.

Subject requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Accounting & auditing8every 2 years 
BehavioralUp to 20every 2 yearsMaximum, not a target. No more than 20 behavioral hours count toward the 80. Under the rule, anything that isn't accounting & auditing, technical business or Florida-approved ethics is behavioral — including communications, personal development and general (non-Florida) ethics courses.
Governmental (Yellow Book audits)24every 2 yearsApplies to: Yellow Book (government) audits. 24 hours of governmental CPE if you work on audits under Government Auditing Standards. This comes from the GAO Yellow Book, not the Florida rule.

Special requirements

RequirementHoursDetails
Florida Board-approved ethics4every 2 years4 hours from a Florida Board-approved ethics provider, covering Chapters 455 and 473, F.S.

New licensees

Your first Florida CPE period runs from your license issue date to the third June 30 after it.

Other limits

  • Self-study in A&A or technical business must be from NASBA QAS sponsors.
  • Authorship: max 20 hours per period, and never toward ethics.
  • Credit in increments of not less than one half hour (50-minute contact hour).

Licensed in more than one state?

Many boards let CPAs whose main office is in another state meet CPE through their home state's rules instead, sometimes with extras such as a state ethics course. Check with the Florida Board of Accountancy (DBPR) whether that applies to you.
